Trip 02 · Malaysia

Twelve days in Malaysia

A city that never cools down, tea hills that never stop raining, and an island that fixed everything.

When

August 2025

Length

12 days

Route

KL → Highlands → Langkawi

Kid rating

10 / 10

Malaysia was the first trip where the kids helped plan the route. Lucía wanted a city, Diego wanted a beach, and Nina wanted "monkeys". We managed all three, in that order, with a rainy detour through the mountains in the middle.

Days 1–4 — Kuala Lumpur

KL hits you with heat the moment the terminal doors open and does not let up. We stayed near Bukit Bintang and learned quickly to do things early, retreat indoors at two, and come back out when the light went gold.

The Petronas Towers were the obvious first stop, but the day everyone remembers was Batu Caves: 272 painted steps, a lot of complaining on the way up, and macaques at the top who stole a whole packet of crackers straight out of Diego's hand. Nina got her monkeys. Diego got a lesson in bag security.

Food was the real reason to stay four nights. Roti canai for breakfast on a plastic stool, char kway teow from a stall in Jalan Alor, and a bowl of laksa so good Elena went back the next night to ask how it was made.

“You do not eat in Kuala Lumpur. You eat five times and then have supper.”

Days 5–7 — Cameron Highlands

Four hours north by car and the temperature dropped fifteen degrees. We wore jumpers for the first time in a week, which felt like a holiday inside a holiday. The tea plantations near Sungai Palas roll over the hills in green corduroy rows, and when the mist comes down you can only see three of them at a time.

We walked a mossy forest trail, drank tea on a terrace in the rain, visited a strawberry farm entirely for the children, and ate steamboat dinner in Tanah Rata, all five of us cooking bits of things in a bubbling pot in the middle of the table.

Days 8–12 — Langkawi

A short flight and we were on the coast. Langkawi is where the trip stopped being a route and became a holiday: mornings in the sea at Tanjung Rhu, an afternoon on the SkyBridge above the treetops, and a boat through the mangroves where an eagle came down for a fish about six metres from the bow.

The last two days we did essentially nothing. Breakfast, swim, nap, swim, sunset from the beach at Cenang with an ice cream in each hand. Diego has already asked when we can go back.

What we'd tell a friend

Twelve days is right for these three stops; ten would have felt rushed. August meant afternoon rain almost daily, which we stopped fighting after day two and started planning around. Bring one warm layer for the Highlands even in summer. Take the cheap internal flight rather than the long drive to the coast — the hours you save go straight into the sea. And eat at the stalls with the longest local queue, every time.
